Buying AI licences is easy. Changing how people work is much harder. Most organisations that roll out AI tools see a short burst of curiosity. After that, a handful of enthusiasts keep using them and everyone else carries on as before.

The usual cause is training that teaches features. A finance manager has little use for a tour of a chatbot. What helps is seeing how the variance note they write every month can be drafted much faster, from their own numbers, with the checks they already trust.

Without a structured programme, adoption stays uneven, people start using AI on personal accounts, and leadership has no way to tell whether any of it is making a difference.
