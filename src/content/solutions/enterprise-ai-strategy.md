Many AI initiatives stall long before the technology becomes the problem. They begin with a tool, such as a chatbot, a copilot licence, or a vendor demo, and then look for somewhere to use it. Six months later there are pilots in three departments, no shared data foundation, and nobody can say what changed in the business.

The opposite pattern is just as common. A long strategy exercise produces a list of use cases with no sequence, no owner, and no link to the numbers leadership already watches.

A useful AI strategy answers a few plain questions. Which decisions and processes should change first? What will it take to change them? How will you know it worked? We keep the work focused on those questions.
